Drawing Hands

A picture that draws itself · after M. C. Escher, Drawing Hands (1948)

How it works

Each hand is half drawing and half solid. Its fingers and pencil are modelled in 3D, lit, and cast shadows on the sheet. Behind the wrist the same arm is pressed down into the paper until it has no height at all, and there it exists only as pencil lines.

Those lines are being drawn by the other hand. Each pencil tip follows a list of strokes: the outline of the wrist, the rim of the cuff, its buttons, the sleeve and then the shading. Each stroke appears exactly where the tip passes. The fingers move the pencil over short distances and the whole hand shifts for longer ones. Both pencils move at the same moment, so neither hand is drawn first.

The two hands are one model placed twice, half a turn apart round the centre of the sheet. Turn the sheet upside down and the arrangement is unchanged. The loop has no first step: A draws B, which draws A. Douglas Hofstadter used Escher's print as his picture of a strange loop.

Tilt the sheet turns the view to the side. The solid hands stand up out of the paper, the cuffs they grow from lie flat, and a red loop traces who draws whom.
