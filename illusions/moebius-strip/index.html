Möbius Lattice

a one-sided surface, after Escher’s Möbius Strip II

Red ant
0°of 720°
At the start mark, right way up

One side, two laps

The band is a real parametric surface, M(σ, t) = c(σ) + t·w(σ). The point c(σ) runs once round an upright ellipse, and the cross-piece w(σ) turns through exactly half a turn on the way. So after one lap, the point (σ + 360°, t) is the point (σ, −t): the band has come back flipped over.

The surface normal, the direction an ant’s back points, flips with it. An ant that walks once round arrives back at its start mark, but on the far face of the strap, upside down. Only after a second lap is it home the right way up. Its journey is 720°, not 360°. The dial counts it: the solid red bar on the strap marks 0°, the outlined one 360°.

Press Paint one side to let paint spread from the red ant along the band, both ways at once. It never crosses an edge. Yet the two fronts meet on the far face, directly behind where they started, and by then every face of every strap is painted. There was only one side to paint. The stripes mark every 30° of travel from the start.

The lattice is cut in the surface’s own coordinates, measured along the surface so each strap keeps its width: two edge straps, the centre strap the ants walk on, and two zigzag braces. The pattern is unchanged when t becomes −t, so it survives the flip without a seam. With nine bays, an odd number, each brace even closes on itself after one lap.
