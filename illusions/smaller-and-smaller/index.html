Smaller and Smaller

Infinity toward a point: a self-similar tiling, after Escher’s Smaller and Smaller (1956) and Square Limit (1964)

How it works

The square is cut into nested square rings. Each ring is half the size of the one outside it. Each ring is a pinwheel of four rectangles, and each rectangle is three squares long. Every dolphin is one of those rectangles with its edges bent. Every ring is an exact half-scale copy of the next, so the dolphins shrink toward the centre and never reach it.

For the bent tiles to fit with no gaps, the edges must agree. A dolphin’s back runs along the outer edge of its ring. The next ring out is twice as big, so the belly of each dolphin there must match the backs and heads of the smaller dolphins below it, at half scale. The shape is built that way: its belly is a half-size copy of the head and back of the ring inside, plus the tail of the dolphin ahead, turned over. Only the back, head and tail can be drawn freely. The fit is then exact for any shape, even while the tails beat.

Light and dark dolphins alternate around each ring and from one ring to the next. Scaling by two and turning a quarter turn maps the whole tiling onto itself, light dolphins onto light. So the zoom turns a quarter turn for every doubling, and the endless spiral is a single seamless loop. Reveal construction straightens the dolphins back into the rectangles and draws the square grid underneath.
