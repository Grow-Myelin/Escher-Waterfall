The Cistern Stair

anamorphic pavement art, after the chalk illusions of Kurt Wenner, Julian Beever and Edgar Mueller

How it works

The pit, the steps, the water and the column do not exist. They are a 3D scene built under the paving and rendered once from a single eye point, 1.65 m above the chalk footprints. That picture is then pasted onto the pavement by projecting it back from the same point.

Each point on the paving and the hidden point behind it lie on one straight line from the eye. They land on the same spot of the eye's retina, so from there the flat drawing and the 3D scene are indistinguishable. The edge of the hole lies on the ground, so it is drawn true to size. Everything below the ground lands between you and its real position. The column top, 0.8 m above the paving, is drawn where the line through it meets the ground, far behind. That is why the column becomes an 8 m smear when seen from above.

Step off the eye point and the lines no longer meet. The far end of the drawing, squeezed into a sliver from the eye, opens out to its real length, and the pit flattens into a long stretched picture on the stones. Pavement artists check their work through a camera on a tripod at the marked spot for the same reason. One eye at one point is the only place the trick works.
