The Sweet Spot

A real object that is a Penrose triangle from one point only, after the Impossible Triangle in East Perth

How it works

The sculpture is three square bars, 4.6 m long, along three perpendicular directions, joined at two right-angled corners. It does not close: the free end of the third bar stops 8.0 m in front of the start of the first. From the bronze marker, both ends lie on one line of sight, and the bars draw a closed triangle in which every corner looks like a right angle.

A single eye can't measure depth along a line of sight. Two surfaces that cover the same directions look the same, however far apart they are. The dashed red line marks that sight line: from the marker it shrinks to a point.

The near end is 17.0 m from the eye and the far end 25.0 m, so a near end of the same size would look too big. Here the near end is an exact copy of the far corner, scaled by 0.68 about the eye. Scaling about the eye moves every point along its own sight line, so from the marker the picture does not change. To meet that smaller end, each bar tapers by 12% (from 64 cm wide at the far end to 44 cm at the near end).

The corner is split along sight lines. The wedge of the first bar that, from the eye, lies in front of the arriving bar is carried forward with the near end. Every line of sight through the corner then meets the same faces, in the same order, as it would at a real joint. So the join is seamless, but only from that one point.
