The Island and the Mirror

a cylindrical-mirror anamorphosis with a double image, after the catoptric prints of the 17th century and István Orosz

How it works

The sheet is an engraving of an island fortress in a lake. Stand the steel cylinder on its tower and look from the marked eye point: the curved walls straighten into a stone Penrose triangle.

The triangle was drawn first, on the picture plane of the eye. Each point of it defines a ray from the eye. The ray strikes the cylinder, and because the cylinder's surface is vertical its normal is horizontal: the ray keeps its downward slope and is mirrored only sideways. Followed down to the paper, it marks where that point of the picture must be printed. Rays from low on the cylinder land close to its foot; rays from near its top travel furthest. So the picture is turned inside out round the cylinder, with its bottom edge wrapped round the foot and its top flung out towards you.

The drawing on the sheet is that map applied to every point, computed exactly. The mirror is ray-traced the same way, so turning away from the eye point shows the real, bent reflection. The fortress, lake, shores and town are drawn only where the mirror does not look. The same stones serve both pictures.
