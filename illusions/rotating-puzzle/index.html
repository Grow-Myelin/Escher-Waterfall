The Turning Bridge

a rotating-world puzzle, after Monument Valley and Echochrome

How the bridge reaches the bell tower

The camera is orthographic and looks straight down the cube diagonal (1, 1, 1). From there, any two points that differ by a multiple of (1, 1, 1) fall on the same spot of the picture. So a tile 2 units higher, 2 nearer on x and 2 nearer on z looks exactly like a tile at the same height.

The crank turns the bridge in quarter turns. At 0° its tip meets the landing at the top of the stairs: a real join. At 270° the tip lines up with the top of the bell tower, which is really 2 units higher and nearer on every axis, about 3.5 tiles closer to you along your line of sight. At 90° it lines up with the garden, 3.5 tiles farther away. The tower bases vanish into mist, so nothing in the picture gives the depths away.

The traveller follows the picture, not the world. Walking off the tip, it reaches the seam and is moved by 2·(1, 1, 1) along your line of sight, which you cannot see. A join only counts when the tiles line up exactly, and when the lower tile in the picture is also the nearer one, so its edge hides the seam. The path finder searches over tiles and bridge positions together, which is how Auto-solve plans its turns.

Reveal the trick swings the camera off the diagonal. The dashed red lines run along the old line of sight and span each gap.
