Square or Round

Ambiguous cylinders in a mirror, after Kokichi Sugihara

How it works

Each tube has straight, vertical walls, but its top edge is not flat: it rises at two corners and dips at the other two. Seen from the front, 42° above the table, the edge lines up exactly with the outline of a flat square. Seen from behind at the same angle, which is the view the mirror gives, it lines up with the outline of a flat circle. The eye takes the simplest reading, a flat rim, so it sees two different tubes.

Both views keep each point's left–right position x. They differ only in how depth turns into height in the picture: s = y cos θ − z sin θ from the front, and s = y cos θ + z sin θ from the back. For every x, setting the first to the square's edge and the second to the circle's edge gives two linear equations. Together they fix one height y and one depth z, and that is the rim.

The result is a rounded square in plan, halfway between the two shapes. Its straight sides run exactly along the mirror's line of sight, so the circle sees each of them as a single point. Turning a tube by 180° swaps front and back, and so swaps the square and the circle. Show the real shape swings the view off the design angle and draws, in red, the flat square and circle and the sight lines that join them to the rim.

The views here are parallel projections, as if seen from far away, so the construction is exact for any tube anywhere on the table. Sugihara builds his objects for a camera at a fixed distance in the same way.
