Three Gravities

multiple gravities in one building, after M. C. Escher’s Relativity (1953)

Three floors at right angles

You look into one corner of a cubic hall, along the cube’s diagonal, with the three near walls cut away. Each of the three back planes is a floor. One world stands on the bottom one and the other two stand on the walls. Every face you can see points up in exactly one of the three worlds, so it is a floor there and a wall in the other two.

The stairs are shared. On each flight one world walks on the treads and the other walks on the risers, so the same steps work from two sides. Two people on a flight can walk in the same direction while one climbs and the other descends. Each lane ends in a doorway that stands upright for its own walker. In the other world the same doorway lies flat, as a hatch in the floor.

The building is one design, built three times. Each copy is rotated 120° about your line of sight, which takes each world’s down to the next one’s. Turn rolls the picture by exactly that angle. When it stops, the hall looks just as it did before, but a different set of people stand upright. Every part is an ordinary place for somebody.
