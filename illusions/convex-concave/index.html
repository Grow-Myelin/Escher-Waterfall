Convex and Concave

the light decides which way round you see it, after Escher (1955) and Schröder (1858)

Floor or ceiling?

In a parallel projection every point slides freely along your line of sight without changing the drawing. Push the whole relief through the picture plane, reversing all its depths, and you get its mould: floors turn into ceilings, a column into a niche, a stair that climbs into one that hangs. The lines are identical. Only the shading differs, because each surface now faces the other way up.

We read shading assuming the light comes from above. A face that is lit reads as turned up, and a face in shadow reads as turned down. Here the arcade is mirror-symmetric about its centre line. The left half is shaded with its real surfaces, the right half with the surfaces of its mould. So the left reads as seen from above and the right as seen from below. Across the middle the relief is flattened and turned over gradually. At the centre line every face points straight at you and the shading says nothing, so the two halves join without a seam.

Swing the light round to come from below and the evidence reverses: the right half becomes solid and the left becomes hollow. With no shading at all, as in Schröder’s line drawing of 1858, the reading flips back and forth by itself.
