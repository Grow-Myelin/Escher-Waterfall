Reflecting Sphere

a mirror ball shows the whole room, and the viewer at its centre · after Hand with Reflecting Sphere (1935)

What the artist sees

Why you are always at the centre

Every ray from your eye that meets the ball bounces off it at the same angle it arrived, measured from the surface normal. On a sphere every normal points straight out from the centre. So the one ray that comes straight back to your eye is the one aimed at the centre, and it meets the ball at the point nearest you, which you see in the middle of the disc. That is where your own eye appears, whichever way you walk round the ball.

The other rays fan out. The further a ray lands from the middle, the more it is turned aside. A ray that just grazes the edge goes on almost straight, so the thin rim of the disc shows what is directly behind the ball. Between the middle and the rim lies the rest of the room, squeezed together. The ball shows almost everything around it, in one picture.

Here every pixel of the ball is computed that way. The eye ray is intersected with the true sphere and reflected about the normal. The reflected ray is then traced through a depth map of the room, captured from the ball's centre. That puts near things, such as the pedestal, the artist and the desk, where they really are.

Press Show the rays to step outside. The red line is the ray that comes back, and the inset shows what the artist sees.
