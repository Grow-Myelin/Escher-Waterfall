Metamorphosis

A tiling that changes along its length, after Escher’s Metamorphosis I–III (1937–1968)

How it works

Wherever you stop the strip, the tiles fit together with no gaps and no overlaps. Yet no two columns are quite the same: the shape of the tiles is a slowly changing function of the position along the strip.

Every tile is built from six shared corners and six shared edges. Each corner and each edge curve is computed once, from the morph values at its own place on the strip, and is then used by the tiles on both sides of it. A bump that grows out of one tile is the same curve as the dent in its neighbour, so the pieces always fit, however quickly the shapes change.

All the tiles have the topology of hexagons, with three meeting at every corner. A square is a hexagon with two opposite edges shrunk to zero length; the point where four squares meet is one of those edges. Sliding each column up by half a row while drawing the columns closer together turns the squares into regular hexagons. Over one loop the columns climb exactly 14 rows, so the strip closes on itself and scrolls forever.
