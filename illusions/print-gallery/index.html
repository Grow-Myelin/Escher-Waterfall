Print Gallery

A picture that contains itself, twisted round its centre · after Escher (1956) and Lenstra & de Smit (2003)

How it works

Start with the straight picture (slide to Straight). A visitor looks at a print of a harbour. At the end of the quay is the gallery building, and its first-floor window frames the same room again, 256 times smaller. The picture repeats inside itself for ever, like a Droste tin.

Now take logarithms. Put w = log z, with z measured from the centre of the repetition. Circles round the centre become vertical lines and rays become horizontal lines. Shrinking by 256 becomes a sideways shift of ln 256, and a full turn becomes a shift of 2πi. In these coordinates the straight picture is a wallpaper that repeats in a rectangular grid. Log plane shows it.

Escher's twist turns and shrinks that wallpaper by the complex number α = (2πi + ln 256) / 2πi, which is a turn of 41.4° and a shrink to 0.75. Then going once round the centre adds 2πi to log z. That moves w = α·log z by 2πi + ln 256, which is a period of the wallpaper, so the picture closes up with no seam. The map z ↦ exp(α log z) is conformal: it keeps small shapes undistorted while bending straight lines into spirals. At any other twist the picture does not close, and the red dashed line marks the seam.

The twisted picture repeats when you zoom by 22.58 and turn by 157.6°. That is what the endless zoom follows. Escher drew by hand and left the centre blank. Here every scale is drawn, so the centre fills itself.
